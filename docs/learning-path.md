# Learning path: Lessons and Phrases

Status: **v1 built** — A1 lessons and phrasebooks for Italian, Spanish, French and Portuguese; level overviews A1–C2 for all four.

## The problem

Everything Langue has today is practice: flashcards, fill-in-the-blank,
translation, reading, conversation. Practice consolidates what you already
understand. It does not teach. A true beginner opening flashcards is memorising
strings with no idea why *il libro* but *lo studente*, or why *sono* means both
"I am" and "they are".

Friends who tried Duolingo report the same thing: guessing the rules from
examples is slow and frustrating for adults. Adults learn faster when the rule
is stated, shown, and then practised — and when they have a stock of
ready-made phrases to get by with while the grammar catches up.

So two new sections, both organised by CEFR level:

1. **Phrases** — a phrasebook of the expressions you need at this level,
   grouped by situation, that you can browse, listen to, and drill both ways.
2. **Lessons** — a short guided path through how the language works at this
   level: one concept per lesson, explained in plain English, then practised.

## The learning loop

Each lesson feeds the activities that already exist, so the app becomes one
loop instead of six separate tools:

| Step | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | 3–5 minutes: the rule in plain English, a table, examples with translations and audio | Lesson |
| **Practise** | 8–12 graded items that use only what has been taught so far | Lesson |
| **Reinforce** | The lesson's words and phrases join your flashcard and phrase review pool | Flashcards, Phrases |
| **Use** | Conversation, translation and reading are told which grammar you have covered, so an A1 learner is not ambushed by a tense they have never seen | Existing activities |

## What each level means, and what it takes in Italian

CEFR levels describe what you can *do*. The exams (CILS, CELI, PLIDA) test
all four skills — reading, writing, listening, speaking — against those
descriptors. The grammar lists below are the usual Italian inventory for each
level, aligned with those exams.

Each level gets an overview page in the app: "what you'll be able to do"
plus a grammar checklist that ticks off as you complete lessons. That page is
the answer to "what do I need to know to pass A1?".

### A1 — Beginner

**You can:** greet people and introduce yourself; ask and answer simple
personal questions (name, origin, where you live, job, age); handle numbers,
prices, times and dates; order food and drink and buy simple things; say what
you have, like, want and can do; describe your family and home in simple
sentences; read signs, menus and short messages; fill in a simple form.

**Grammar:** pronunciation and spelling · subject pronouns, *tu* vs *Lei* ·
*essere* and *avere* · *c'è / ci sono* · noun gender and plural · articles
(*il, lo, l', la, i, gli, le*; *un, uno, una, un'*) · adjective agreement ·
*questo* · present tense of regular *-are, -ere, -ire* and *-isc-* verbs ·
core irregulars (*fare, andare, venire, stare*) · modals (*volere, potere,
dovere*) and polite *vorrei* · negation · question words · possessives ·
*piacere* · reflexive verbs for daily routine · prepositions and their
article contractions (*al, nel, del, sul, dal*) · numbers, time, dates.

**Themes:** greetings, personal details, family, food and cafés, shopping,
town and directions, home, daily routine, free time.

### A2 — Elementary

**You can:** say what you did last weekend and what you used to do as a
child; make plans and arrangements; handle routine transactions (transport,
hotels, clothes shopping, pharmacy); describe people, places and your
background; write short notes and simple personal messages.

**Grammar:** *passato prossimo* with *avere* and *essere*, participle
agreement, irregular participles · *imperfetto* and when to use it instead ·
reflexive verbs in the past · direct and indirect object pronouns · *ci*
(there) and *ne* (of it) · *futuro semplice* · *stare* + gerund · comparatives
and superlatives (*più … di/che*, *-issimo*, *migliore*) · partitive (*del pane*)
· informal imperative · conditional for politeness (*potrei, mi piacerebbe*) ·
*fa*, *da* + present · *sapere* vs *conoscere*.

**Themes:** travel and holidays, transport, clothes, health, weather, house,
work and study, childhood memories.

### B1 — Intermediate

**You can:** deal with most travel situations, including problems; describe
experiences, hopes and plans, and give reasons for your opinions; follow the
main points of clear TV and radio on familiar topics; tell a story or the plot
of a film; write connected text about experiences and impressions.

**Grammar:** conditional (present and past) · subjunctive present and past
(*penso che*, *spero che*, *è importante che*) · *trapassato prossimo* ·
*futuro anteriore* and the future of probability · combined pronouns (*glielo,
me ne*) · formal imperative and imperative with pronouns (*dimmi*) · relative
pronouns (*che, cui*) · pronominal verbs (*andarsene, farcela, metterci*) ·
gerund and infinitive constructions · "if" sentences of reality and possibility ·
recognising *passato remoto* · connectors (*però, quindi, siccome, anche se*).

**Themes:** work and careers, education, media, environment, relationships,
feelings, culture.

### B2 — Upper intermediate

**You can:** understand complex texts, including technical discussion in your
field; follow news and films; talk with native speakers without strain on
either side; argue a viewpoint with pros and cons; write clear, detailed
essays, reports and formal letters.

**Grammar:** imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive, sequence of tenses · all
three "if" constructions · the passive (*essere/venire/andare* + participle,
*si passivante*) · impersonal *si* · reported speech · *passato remoto* in
narration · implicit clauses (*finito il lavoro…*) · conjunctions that take
the subjunctive (*benché, affinché, purché, a meno che*) · indefinites ·
word formation (*-ino, -one, -accio*).

**Themes:** society, politics, economy, science and technology, the arts,
debate.

### C1 — Advanced

**You can:** understand long, demanding texts and what is implied; express
yourself fluently without searching for words; switch register for social,
academic and professional use; write well-structured text on complex subjects.

**Grammar and skills:** subtle mood choice (subjunctive in relative and
independent clauses: *cerco qualcuno che sappia…*, *magari!*) · cleft and
dislocated sentences (*il caffè lo prendo amaro*) · formal and bureaucratic
style · idiomatic pronominal verbs (*prendersela, cavarsela*) · discourse
markers (*tuttavia, pertanto, dal momento che*) · register and regional
variation.

### C2 — Mastery

**You can:** understand virtually everything; summarise and reconstruct
arguments from several sources; express fine shades of meaning precisely.

**Grammar and skills:** literary forms (*passato remoto*, *trapassato remoto*,
*egli/esso*) · style, irony and rhetoric · proverbs, idioms and cultural
reference · specialised registers (legal, academic, journalistic).

### An honest gap

The exams test listening and speaking. Langue is text-first. v1 adds a
"hear it" button on every phrase, example and lesson word, using the
browser's built-in speech synthesis — free, offline, and good on Apple
devices, uneven elsewhere.

**Recorded audio (next):** generate each phrase and example once with a
high-quality TTS voice, store the clips in the R2 bucket keyed by a hash of
language + text, and serve them from there. `SpeakButton` already takes a
`src`: when a clip exists it plays the clip, and falls back to the browser
voice if the clip fails to load. Nothing is synthesised per request.
Speaking practice via speech recognition works only in some browsers, so it
comes later.

## Feature 1 — Phrases

A phrasebook per level, organised by situation. A1 Italian is about 75
phrases in nine situations:

| Situation | e.g. |
|---|---|
| Greetings and politeness | *Buongiorno · Grazie mille · Prego · Scusi · Mi dispiace* |
| Meeting people | *Come ti chiami? · Piacere! · Di dove sei? · Che lavoro fai?* |
| When you don't understand | *Non capisco · Può ripetere? · Più lentamente, per favore · Come si dice…?* |
| Café and restaurant | *Vorrei un cappuccino · Il conto, per favore · Sono vegetariana* |
| Shopping | *Quanto costa? · Sto solo guardando · Posso provarlo?* |
| Getting around | *Dov'è la stazione? · È lontano? · Sempre dritto · Un biglietto per Roma* |
| Time and plans | *Che ore sono? · Ci vediamo domani! · Va bene · D'accordo* |
| Help and emergencies | *Aiuto! · Ho bisogno di un medico · Dov'è la farmacia?* |
| Small talk | *Che bello! · Che tempo fa? · Fa freddo · Buon compleanno!* |

Each phrase carries:

- the Italian, plus genuinely interchangeable variants (*Sono americano / americana*);
- the English, plus acceptable alternatives;
- a usage note where it matters ("informal — with strangers say *Come sta?*");
- a **literal breakdown**: *Come ti chiami?* — "how yourself (you) call?".
  This is what turns a memorised string into an understood structure, and it
  quietly previews the grammar the lessons will teach.

**Modes:**

- **Browse** — the phrasebook, with a "hear it" button per phrase.
- **Practise** — flashcard-style typing, in either direction (Italian → English
  or English → Italian), for one situation or mixed.
- **Review** — the phrases you missed recently.

**Grading** is local and instant, with no API calls, using the same engine as
flashcards. It ignores punctuation and capitals and accepts every listed variant. Accents get special
handling: *e* for *è* is a real error (it means "and" instead of "is"), so an
accent-only slip is graded *close* with a note rather than *wrong* or silently
*right*. Typing accents on an English keyboard is awkward, so every
target-language input gets a row of accent buttons (à è é ì ò ù).

Each phrase's results are tracked like a word, so spaced review can schedule
it later.

## Feature 2 — Lessons

A level is a path of 12–16 short lessons, 10–15 minutes each. One concept per
lesson, plus the words needed to use it.

**Lesson screen:**

1. **Goal** — "After this lesson you can: say who you are and where you're from."
2. **Learn** — 2–4 short sections: the rule in plain English, a table where
   one helps, examples with translations and audio, and "watch out" tips aimed
   at English speakers.
3. **New words** — the lesson's vocabulary.
4. **Practise** — 8–12 items: fill in the right form, translate in both
   directions, pick the right option. Each answer gets a one-line *why*.
5. **Done** — score; 70% or more marks the lesson complete, and its words and
   phrases join your review pool.

Plus **Ask about this lesson** — a box where the AI tutor answers questions with the lesson as
context ("why *gli amici* and not *i amici*?"). That is the only model call
in a lesson.

**Level overview** — can-do list, grammar checklist with ticks, lesson list
with "next up". Nothing is locked: the order is a recommendation, not a
gate. Adults skip what they know.

**Level check** — at the end of a level, 20 mixed items drawn from every
lesson. 80% or more suggests moving up.

### Italian A1 path

| # | Lesson | After it you can… |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ciao! | greet people at any time of day, introduce yourself, ask a name — informally and politely (*tu* vs *Lei*) |
| 2 | How Italian sounds | read any word aloud: *c/g* before *e/i*, *ch/gh*, *gli*, *gn*, *sc*, double consonants, stress |
| 3 | Io sono | use *essere* and the subject pronouns; say where you're from; make negatives and yes/no questions |
| 4 | Il, lo, la | tell masculine from feminine, make plurals, pick the right "the" and "a" |
| 5 | Ho fame | use *avere* for having, age (*ho 30 anni*) and feelings (*ho fame*); say what there is (*c'è / ci sono*) |
| 6 | Numbers and time | count to 1,000, ask prices, tell the time, give dates |
| 7 | Parlo italiano | conjugate regular *-are* verbs; ask questions with *chi, cosa, dove, quando, come, perché* |
| 8 | Leggo, dormo, capisco | conjugate *-ere*, *-ire* and *-isc-* verbs |
| 9 | Bello e buono | make adjectives agree and place them; use *questo* |
| 10 | La mia famiglia | use possessives, including why it is *mia madre* but *la mia famiglia* |
| 11 | Vado, faccio, sto | use *andare, fare, stare, venire*; say where you're going (*a Roma, in Italia, a casa*) |
| 12 | Vorrei un caffè | use *volere, potere, dovere*; order politely |
| 13 | Mi piace | say what you like with *piacere* — and why it is *mi piacciono* for plurals |
| 14 | La mia giornata | describe your day with reflexive verbs, times and frequency |
| 15 | Al bar, nel centro | combine prepositions with articles (*al, alla, nel, dei, sul*) |

Every lesson's practice items use only grammar from that lesson and the ones
before it.

### Sample: lesson 4, *Il, lo, la*

> **Every noun is masculine or feminine.** Usually *-o* is masculine
> (*il libro*), *-a* is feminine (*la casa*), and *-e* can be either
> (*il pane*, *la notte*) — learn those with their article.
>
> **Plurals change the last vowel:** *-o → -i*, *-a → -e*, *-e → -i*.
> *libro → libri, casa → case, notte → notti.*
>
> **"The" depends on the sound that follows:**
>
> | | singular | plural | before |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | masculine | il | i | most consonants — *il libro, i libri* |
> | masculine | lo | gli | *s* + consonant, *z*, *gn*, *ps* — *lo studente, gli studenti* |
> | masculine | l' | gli | a vowel — *l'amico, gli amici* |
> | feminine | la | le | a consonant — *la casa, le case* |
> | feminine | l' | le | a vowel — *l'amica, le amiche* |
>
> **Watch out:** *un amico* (no apostrophe — masculine) but *un'amica*.
>
> *Practise:* "___ studente" (the) → **lo** · plural of *il ragazzo* →
> **i ragazzi** · "the friends" (male) → **gli amici** · *zaino* takes
> *il* or *lo*? → **lo**, because it starts with *z*.

## Where content comes from

**Lessons and phrases are written content, stored in the repo, not
generated on the fly.**

- Grammar explanations have to be *right*, and consistent from one session to
  the next. A model writing the *lo/gli* rule fresh each time will eventually
  write it wrong, and a beginner cannot tell.
- Ordering matters: lesson 7's practice may only use what lessons 1–6 taught.
  That needs a curated sequence.
- It is free and instant at runtime, and works when the API is down.

The model does what it is good at: answering questions about a lesson,
generating extra practice on demand (constrained to grammar already covered),
and running lesson-themed conversations.

Claude drafts the content; a native speaker's review pass before it is called
finished would be worth arranging. A "report a problem" link on each lesson
lets learners flag mistakes.

## How it is built

- **Content:** `data/curriculum/<language>/<level>/` containing `level.yaml`
  (can-do list, grammar checklist, themes), `phrases.yaml`, and
  `lessons/NN-slug.yaml`. Readable, reviewable diffs.
- **Build:** like vocabulary, compiled at build time into
  `web/src/lib/curriculum/generated.ts` and validated with zod. Malformed
  content fails the build instead of a lesson page.
- **Database:** one new table, `LessonProgress` (user, lesson, best score,
  attempts, completed at). Phrase results reuse `WordStat`.
- **Pages:** `/learn/lessons` (level overview), `/learn/lessons/[id]`,
  `/learn/phrases`. The menu splits into **Learn** (Lessons, Phrases) and
  **Practise** (the existing five), with a "Continue: lesson 4" card at the top.
- **API:** answers stay on the server, as with flashcards — the browser gets
  prompts, and posts answers for grading. `/api/lessons/ask` for tutor
  questions.
- **Languages without content yet** show the overview's "coming soon" state;
  flashcards and conversation keep working for every language.

## Rollout

1. **Framework + A1 in all four languages** — 15 lessons and ~75 phrases each
   for Italian, Spanish, French and Portuguese (Brazilian); level overviews
   (can-do + grammar checklists) A1–C2 for each; browser audio; accent
   buttons. Multiple-choice flashcards (the default) ship alongside, for
   younger learners.
2. **Hook into practice** — "words from my lessons" flashcard deck; lesson
   scenarios in conversation; covered-grammar constraints in generated
   exercises; level check.
3. **A2** in each language, and recorded audio from R2.
4. Later: pictures on vocabulary cards, speaking practice, spaced-repetition
   review of phrases.
