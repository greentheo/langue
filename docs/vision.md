# Langue — vision

**Langue helps people actually read, write and speak a language correctly, at
real, recognised levels, a few minutes a day, without tricks.**

Read this before making a product decision. When a feature is in doubt, ask
which of the promises below it serves, and whether it breaks a principle.

## Why another language app

Most language apps are built to make you *feel* fluent. You tap words in a
word bank, typos and missing accents are waved through, a level is whatever
the app says it is, and a lost streak is the stick that keeps you coming back.
People come out able to recognise a language but not produce it: sloppy
spelling, guessed grammar, and little confidence in a real conversation. (Our
founder's French, after years of the market leader, was exactly that.)

Langue is built the other way round: **what you produce is what counts.**

## Who it's for

People who want to *use* a language — travel, family, heritage, work, a move
abroad — and are willing to spend 20–30 minutes a day on it. Adults and kids
from about ten. Small, private groups of family and friends who keep each
other going.

## What makes it different

1. **Accuracy first.** You type, build and say real answers, and the details
   count: accents and diacritics, agreement, verb endings, cases, particles,
   tones, characters — whatever "spelled right" means in the language. A miss
   is explained, not just marked. A typo is forgiven; a wrong ending is not,
   because the ending is the grammar.
2. **Your mistakes, made visible.** Langue keeps track of the *kinds* of
   mistakes you make — gender agreement, the past tense's helper verb, a
   tone, a particle, a spelling pattern — shows you your top few, and drills
   them. Nobody improves at what they can't see.
3. **Listen, then write it down.** Dictation trains the gap between how a
   language sounds and how it's written: French spelling, Italian double
   consonants, Portuguese nasal vowels, Chinese tones, Japanese kana. It
   costs nothing to run.
4. **Speak it.** Scripted role-plays from the first week, answered by
   picking, then typing, then saying the reply out loud; shadowing for sounds
   that don't exist in English; later, an AI conversation partner that
   corrects you.
5. **Write it.** Short writing tasks at every level, corrected line by line —
   the one place where an AI model earns its cost.
6. **Real levels.** Levels follow the international frameworks — the CEFR
   (A1–C2) for European languages, and each language's own standard where it
   has one: HSK for Chinese, JLPT for Japanese, TOPIK for Korean. Practice
   sections modelled on the official exams (DELF, CILS, DELE, Celpe-Bras,
   Goethe, HSK, JLPT, TOPIK…) mean "B1 complete" is a claim you could test.
7. **Honest motivation.** Streaks are never taken away; reminders come after
   a couple of quiet days, never daily; no lives or hearts that stop you
   learning; no ads, no selling data, no paywalled basics. Groups are private.
   Progress bars only ever go up.

## How we build it

- **Content and algorithms first; AI where only AI will do.** The learning
  loop — what to learn next, when to review, how hard to push, how to grade —
  is hand-written content and bookkeeping, unit-tested and free to run. A
  model is for what nothing else can do: correcting free writing, open
  conversation, explaining an unusual mistake. Always optional, always
  capped, always the cheapest model that is good enough.
- **A guided path, ~3 minutes a step.** One button plans the next step:
  something new, reviews that are due, the things you keep missing, a stretch.
  Steps climb from recognising to recalling to producing to using, so
  practice gets harder by itself. Tools for picking your own activity stay
  available, never required.
- **Progress you can trust.** Mastery over time-spent; spaced review to
  confirm it; a level means you can do what the level's can-do list says.
- **Content is data, and it is tested.** Lessons, phrases, role-plays and
  vocabulary are files reviewed like code. Tests check every listed answer
  grades correct, nothing is taught twice, every exercise can be shown. The
  variety we teach is explicit (Brazilian Portuguese; Spanish understood on
  both sides of the Atlantic) and native-quality.
- **Small, private, kind.** Invite-only groups, minimal personal data, no
  dark patterns. Built for kids as well as adults.

## Beyond the Romance languages

Langue starts with Italian, Spanish, French and Portuguese, and should grow
to German, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, African languages such as Swahili,
Yoruba or Zulu, and more. The design has to hold for all of them:

- **Writing systems.** Latin with diacritics (where accents carry meaning, as
  in Vietnamese or Yoruba tone marks, they are never stripped when grading);
  other alphabets (Cyrillic, Greek); right-to-left scripts (Arabic, Hebrew);
  syllabaries and characters (Japanese kana and kanji, Chinese hanzi, Korean
  hangul). An item may carry several written forms — native script,
  romanisation (pinyin, romaji, Revised Romanisation) and readings
  (furigana) — and the ladder steps from romanisation to native script:
  recognise, recall, then type it with the phone's own keyboard or input
  method.
- **Sound.** Tones and pitch accent are graded, not decoration; audio comes
  first for tonal languages. Device voices are good for some languages and
  missing for others, so recorded audio is part of the plan.
- **Grammar that isn't Romance.** Cases (German, Slavic), particles and
  honorifics (Japanese, Korean), measure words (Chinese), noun classes (Bantu
  languages). Lessons-as-sections, the stage ladder and mistake categories
  fit them all; graders are written per language, not assumed.
- **Levels.** CEFR where it applies, the native framework where one exists,
  mapped to each other so a family learning different languages can compare.

German is the natural first step beyond Romance (Latin script, new grammar);
Japanese or Korean the first new script.

## What we won't do

- Ads, data selling, or pay-to-keep-learning.
- A chatbot with a lesson veneer, or AI in the core loop by default.
- Threats that come true: nobody loses a streak or progress for taking a break.
- Levels that don't mean anything outside the app.

## How we'll know it's working

- Learners at a level can pass that level's official exam sections.
- Written accuracy rises: mistakes by category go down over weeks.
- People come back on most days without being nagged daily.
- The family actually talks — in Italian at dinner, in French on the trip.
